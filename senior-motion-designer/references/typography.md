# Motion Typography

## Typography is imagery

Type is not merely information placed over animation. It can be the primary visual material.

## Hierarchy

Control:
- font family,
- weight,
- size,
- line height,
- tracking,
- case,
- alignment,
- color,
- line breaks.

A single emphasized word is often stronger than making an entire sentence bold or large.

## Line breaking

Break lines by meaning.

Avoid line breaks that separate tightly connected phrases.

Prefer compact phrases over long subtitle-like paragraphs.

## Kinetic type

Animate groups based on meaning.

Possible behaviors:
- word replaces word,
- sentence expands from a keyword,
- type becomes a mask,
- word physically demonstrates its meaning,
- line reorganizes into diagram/UI,
- type becomes an object for the next scene.

## Character animation

Character-by-character animation is appropriate for:
- precise title construction,
- terminal/typing concepts,
- rhythmic editorial motion,
- intentional typographic reveals.

Do not use it as a default entrance.

## Readability

Never sacrifice legibility for motion.

Ensure enough hold time after movement.

Avoid excessive motion blur on small text.

Avoid scaling text through unreadable sizes during key spoken information.
